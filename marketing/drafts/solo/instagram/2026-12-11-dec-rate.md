# Carousel: Salary maths vs sole trader maths

## Caption
Same $80,000 target. About $40 an hour or about $112 an hour?

Salary maths divides your income by the hours in a year. It works for an employee because someone else covers the gaps. When you work for yourself, the gaps are yours.

Swipe to see what builds the number up. These are illustrative figures, so swap in your own. We used 12% for super as an example. Check the current rate with the ATO.

Save this for when you set your rates for the new year.

We send short, practical emails on pricing, quoting and getting paid, plus new tools and the occasional offer. They're free, and you can unsubscribe any time. Link in bio.

General information only, not financial or tax advice. For tax or super questions, ask the ATO or a registered tax agent.

## Carousel slides
1. Cover: "Salary maths vs sole trader maths". Sub: "Same $80,000 before tax."
2. Salary maths: $80,000 / 1,976 hours (52 weeks x 38) = about $40 an hour.
3. Add super (12% as an example; check the current rate with the ATO) = about $45.
4. Add business costs ($12,000 a year) = about $51.
5. Take out 6 weeks off = about $58.
6. Count only billable hours (60% of working time) = about $97.
7. Add a 15% buffer = $112 (rounded up).
8. Close: "Not a take-home figure. It's before tax. Use your own numbers." Sub: "Free pricing tips: link in bio."
