# Does your rate cover super? (email to consented subscribers)

Audience: only people who signed up to the pricing-tips list and consented to emails from The Solo Service Provider. Do not send to anyone else.

## Subject
Does your rate cover super?

## Preview text
Five things an hourly rate has to cover, in one short list.

## Body

Hi,

Many sole traders work out a rate the way an employee would: take the income you want before tax, divide by the hours in a year, and call it done. With $80,000 and a 38-hour week, that comes to about $40 an hour.

The trouble is that an employee has others covering the gaps. When you work for yourself, the gaps are yours. Here are the five things your rate has to cover:

1. Super. Nobody pays it on top for a sole trader, so if you want to put money aside, it has to come out of what you charge.
2. Business costs. Vehicle, tools, phone, insurance, software and anything else paid before you earn a dollar.
3. Time off. Holidays, public holidays and sick days don't pay.
4. Hours you can't bill. Quoting, travel, admin, invoicing and finding the next job. In our example only 60% of working hours are billable.
5. A buffer. Jobs get postponed, quotes go quiet and invoices arrive late.

In our worked example (illustrative figures: $80,000 before tax, 12% for super, $12,000 a year in costs, 6 weeks off, 60% billable, 15% buffer), the rate goes from about $40 an hour to about $112 an hour. Swap in your own numbers and see where you land.

This isn't a take-home figure, because it doesn't include income tax.
