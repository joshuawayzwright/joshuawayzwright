# Does your rate cover super?

A common way to undercharge: leaving super out of your rate.

As an employee, super gets added on top of your pay. As a sole trader, no employer is paying it for you. If you want it, it has to come out of what you charge.

Here's the sum, using an example target of $80,000 a year before tax:

$80,000 x 12% = $9,600 for super
$80,000 + $9,600 = $89,600

So your rate has to be built on $89,600, not $80,000. That's before business costs, time off and the hours you can't bill.

Whether you put super aside, and how much, is your call. The 12% is only an example. For your own situation, check with the ATO or a registered tax agent.
